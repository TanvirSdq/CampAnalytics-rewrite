Tool 01 Campaign Performance Dynamics
Go To Tool →

The Campaign Performance Dynamics module provides empirical observability into volunteer mobilization, multi-year longitudinal trajectories, and Year-over-Year (YoY) velocity without subjective scoring indices, arbitrary weights, or automated advice notes:

Step-by-Step Tool Usage & Exploration Process

  1. Target Campaign Code: Enter the official identifier for the campaign edition you want to evaluate (e.g. wlmbd24 for Wiki Loves Monuments 2024 Bangladesh, or wlede23 for Wiki Loves Earth Germany).
  2. Baseline Reference Edition: Select Previous Year Baseline to measure trajectory shifts against the immediately preceding year (e.g. 2023 vs 2024), or specify a custom baseline edition for multi-year comparisons.
  3. Regional Peer Calibration: The tool automatically identifies your country's regional cluster (e.g. South Asia, Northern & Western Europe, ESEAP). Regional peer medians are computed across active campaigns to provide context without artificial rankings.
  4. Empirical KPIs & Velocity Badges: Review the 6 core observational cards showing live totals alongside Year-over-Year velocity badges indicating exact net shifts (+/-) and percentage progression.
  5. Interactive Manipulative Charts (Plotly.js): Explore three dynamic visualizers:
    • Multi-Year Longitudinal Trajectory: Dual-axis interactive chart tracking active uploaders, Commons submissions, and quality honors across up to 5 consecutive editions.
    • Cohort Retention & Newcomer Waterfall: Stacked breakdown illustrating the operational balance between returning volunteers and recruited newcomers.
    • Regional Peer Context Comparison: Interactive bar chart comparing your campaign against active regional peers across selectable metrics.
  6. Export Empirical Report: Export raw, non-graded observational records in Wikitext, Markdown, or CSV formats for community reports and affiliate documentation.

Year-over-Year Progression

Rather than grading affiliates with subjective scores, the platform tracks simple, observable changes between consecutive campaign editions:

\[ \text{Year-over-Year Net Shift} = \text{Target Edition Count} - \text{Baseline Edition Count} \] \[ \text{Year-over-Year Change (\%)} = \frac{\text{Target Edition Count} - \text{Baseline Edition Count}}{\text{Baseline Edition Count}} \times 100\% \]
  • Community Scale: Net change in unique photographers submitting at least one eligible photograph.
  • Contributor Retention: Percentage of previous-year participants who returned to upload in the current edition.
  • Newcomer Recruitment: Number of first-time participants who had not uploaded in earlier recorded editions.
  • Content Utility: Number and percentage of submitted photographs actively illustrating Wikipedia articles or Wikidata items.
  • Quality Recognition: Number and percentage of photos receiving formal community honors (Quality Images and Featured Pictures).
  • Contributor Diversity: Distribution of submissions across participants, tracked via the share of photos from the top 10% most prolific uploaders.

Regional Benchmark & Zero-Synthetic Invariant

Regional peer medians provide context across up to 5 active national campaigns within the same geographic cluster, including the target country when held.

\[ \text{Regional Comparison Shift} = \text{Target Value} - \text{Regional Median (Top 5 Active Campaigns)} \]

Zero-Synthetic Invariant & Unheld Campaign Policy: The engine evaluates 100% empirical Wikimedia Commons data without synthetic imputation or mock fallbacks. Unheld editions (e.g. wlmbd21, wlmfr23) are explicitly flagged as unheld and excluded from peer benchmarks rather than being assigned artificial zeros or regional averages.

Tool 02 Retention
Go To Tool →

The Retention Analytics interface evaluates participant continuity between successive campaign editions and cross-event contributor migration:

  1. Campaign Selection: Select one or more configured Wiki Loves campaigns (such as Monuments wlm, Earth wle, Folklore wlf, Africa wla, or Bangla wlb). Query codes follow the standard format [event][country][year] (e.g. wlmbd24, wlede22).
  2. Geographic & Temporal Parameters: Select a target country, regional cluster, or all available countries (50+ global nations), and define the longitudinal year span. Use the Replace Codes or Append Codes buttons to populate the target codes input.
  3. Quick Presets: Pre-configured comparison vectors are available in the presets panel for common evaluation pairs (e.g. Monuments Germany, Earth India, Folklore Bangladesh).
  4. Analytical Projection Models: Choose the desired visualization output:
    • Data Table: Tabular breakdown of baseline cohort size, target cohort size, intersecting participant count, and directional retention rate.
    • Heatmap Matrix: Color-coded matrix displaying pairwise retention shares across consecutive editions.
    • Choropleth World Map: Spatial distribution of country-level average or median retention metrics across international campaigns.

Contributor Retention Rate

Retention measures what fraction of contributors from an earlier baseline edition returned to participate in a subsequent target edition:

\[ \text{Retention Rate} = \frac{\text{Returning Contributors from Baseline}}{\text{Total Contributors in Baseline Edition}} \times 100\% \]

Retention is directional: comparing baseline participants who returned in a later year yields a different percentage than evaluating the later year against the earlier baseline, because total community sizes differ across years.

Data Extraction Pipeline & Limitations

Data is extracted in real time directly from Wikimedia Commons. The primary ingestion pathway queries Toolforge replica tools (ptools.toolforge.org) for low-latency category membership enumeration, with automatic failover to the official Wikimedia Commons Action API (commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php) utilizing the categorymembers generator with automatic pagination and exponential backoff.

  • Evaluations reflect observable on-wiki upload records and do not account for offline organizing contributions.
  • Incomplete or unheld campaign categories are identified as missing data and excluded from evaluation to prevent erroneous zero-scoring.
Tool 03 Influx
Go To Tool →

The Influx Analytics module assesses continuous community renewal, newcomer acquisition, and long-term contributor mobilization:

  1. Series Selection: Choose a campaign type (or All Campaigns), country, and consecutive year span (e.g. {{ DEFAULT_START_YEAR }}–{{ CURRENT_YEAR }}).
  2. Generated Campaign Sequence: Chronological campaign sequence automatically assembled or customized to evaluate longitudinal trends.
  3. Trend Visualizations & Outputs:
    • Summary KPI Cards: Cumulative Contributor Pool, Peak Influx Year, Peak Overall Edition, and Contributor Longevity (recurring participants).
    • Visual Influx Dynamics: Dual-axis stacked bar chart showing first-time newcomers stacked with returning participants alongside the cumulative pool trajectory.
    • Cohort Lifecycle Segmentation: Categorization into 1-Time Entrants (one-off contributors), Repeaters (2–3 editions), and Core Veterans (4+ editions).
    • Detailed Data Table: Comprehensive table with YoY Net Growth, Newcomer Share, Direct Retention, and CSV export capability.

Year-over-Year Newcomers & Retention

In each edition of a campaign series, contributors are divided into two distinct groups: first-time newcomers and returning participants:

\[ \text{Newcomer Share} = \frac{\text{First-Time Contributors}}{\text{Total Active Contributors in Edition}} \times 100\% \] \[ \text{Direct Retention Rate} = \frac{\text{Returning Contributors from Prior Edition}}{\text{Total Contributors in Prior Edition}} \times 100\% \]

First-Time Contributors: Photographers who have never uploaded to any prior recorded edition in the series.
Returning Contributors: Photographers who previously uploaded in at least one earlier recorded edition.

All-Campaigns De-duplication

When analyzing all campaigns together (using the all[country][year] option), participants across all active photography contests (such as Monuments, Earth, Folklore, Africa, and Bangla) in a given year are combined into a single ecosystem pool:

\[ \text{Annual Active Community} = \text{Count of Unique Individuals Active Across Any Contest in that Year} \]

Each volunteer username is counted once per year, eliminating double-counting when a photographer submits images to multiple contests during the same calendar year.

Cumulative Contributor Pool

The cumulative community pool tracks the total number of distinct individuals who have contributed at least one photo across the entire evaluated series up to that edition year:

\[ \text{Cumulative Contributor Pool} = \text{Total Unique Photographers Mobilized Across All Editions to Date} \]
Tool 04 Contributor Mobility
Go To Tool →

The Contributor Mobility module analyzes inter-competition participant fluidity, cross-campaign retention, and community overlap across active photography contests within the same national or regional ecosystem:

  1. Country & Year Selection: Select a target nation and campaign year. The tool identifies all active competitions configured for that country (e.g. WLM, WLE, WLF, WLA, WLB) respecting regional scopes.
  2. Cross-Campaign Matrix: A multi-contest matrix displaying the percentage of contributors from each source campaign who transitioned into participating in a target campaign.
  3. Community Similarity (Jaccard Index): Calculates bidirectional community cohesion, measuring the shared contributor core relative to the joint participant pool.
  4. Deduplicated Ecosystem Pool: Measures the true net distinct volunteer photographers mobilized across all campaigns in that country during the year.

Cross-Campaign Conversion Rate

Measures the proportion of contributors from a source contest who also participated in a target contest in the same country:

\[ \text{Conversion Rate} = \frac{\text{Contributors Participating in Both Contests}}{\text{Total Contributors in Source Contest}} \times 100\% \]

Conversion is directional: the percentage of Earth contributors who entered Monuments is usually different from the percentage of Monuments contributors who entered Earth.

Shared Community Overlap (Jaccard Index)

Quantifies how closely aligned two contributor communities are by comparing shared participants to the total combined group:

\[ \text{Community Overlap (\%)} = \frac{\text{Shared Contributors Active in Both Contests}}{\text{Total Unique Contributors Across Either Contest}} \times 100\% \]

National Ecosystem Reach

The total net number of unique photographers mobilized across all active photography contests within the country during that year:

\[ \text{Total National Ecosystem Reach} = \text{Unique Individuals Participating in At Least One National Contest} \]
Tool 05 Contributor Diversity
Go To Tool →

The Contributor Diversity module evaluates upload volume distribution, contribution inequality, and power-law concentration across contest participants without arbitrary normative baselines:

  1. Campaign & Year Range: Select campaign type (or All Campaigns), target country, and chronological year range.
  2. Normalized Tier Segmentation: Groups participants into four empirical volume tiers: Casual (1–4 uploads), Regular (5–24 uploads), Active (25–99 uploads), and Power (100+ uploads).
  3. Interactive Concentration Trajectory: An interactive line chart plotting the multi-year upload share for any selected top contributor percentile (1%–100%) with automated playback.
  4. Empirical Equality Metrics: Computes the Gini inequality coefficient (0.0 to 1.0) and top-decile shares (top 10% and top 20%).

Contribution Equality (Gini Scale)

The Gini coefficient measures how evenly photo submissions are distributed across all participating uploaders, mapped on a scale from 0.0 to 1.0:

\[ \text{Gini Index} = \text{Distribution inequality of submissions on a scale from 0.0 (equal) to 1.0 (concentrated)} \]
  • 0.0 (Perfect Equality): Every participant submitted exactly the same number of photos.
  • Close to 1.0 (High Concentration): A small group of prolific power uploaders submitted the vast majority of files.

Top 10% Contributor Share

Quantifies what percentage of all contest submissions were uploaded by the top 10% most prolific participants:

\[ \text{Top 10\% Share} = \frac{\text{Photos Uploaded by Top 10\% Most Prolific Contributors}}{\text{Total Contest Submissions}} \times 100\% \]

Percentile Concentration

For any selected tier (such as the top 1%, 5%, or 20%), the platform calculates the exact proportion of submissions generated by that cohort:

\[ \text{Percentile Share} = \frac{\text{Photos Uploaded by Top Selected Group}}{\text{Total Contest Submissions}} \times 100\% \]
Tool 06 Content Utility
Go To Tool →

The Content Utility module evaluates the direct encyclopedic deployment of campaign media across all Wikimedia projects via live prop=globalusage API queries:

  1. Target Campaign Selection: Choose a campaign, country, and edition year (e.g. wlmde24 or wlmbd23).
  2. Usage Harvesting & Namespace Filtering: Analyzes uploaded files to identify whether they are embedded in encyclopedic articles (Mainspace 0) or user/discussion pages.
  3. Analytics & Output Breakdown:
    • Overall Utility Rate (%): Percentage of uploaded files actively utilized in at least one Wikimedia project.
    • Article Inclusions: Cumulative count of live article instances illustrating encyclopedic knowledge.
    • Cross-Wiki Distribution: Distribution across Wikipedia language editions (e.g. English, German, French, Bengali), Wikidata, Wikivoyage, and Commons galleries.
    • Contributor Impact Leaderboard: Top photographers ranked by total downstream article usage.

Content Utility Rate

Tracks how effectively campaign submissions are put to use across Wikipedia articles, Wikidata, and sister projects:

\[ \text{Content Utility Rate} = \frac{\text{Uploaded Photos in Active Use}}{\text{Total Contest Submissions}} \times 100\% \] \[ \text{Total Article Inclusions} = \text{Cumulative Count of Live Embeddings Across All Wikimedia Projects} \]
Tool 07 Quality Recognition
Go To Tool →

The Quality Recognition module audits formal community quality designations awarded to campaign submissions through Wikimedia Commons community review processes:

  1. Campaign Query: Select target event, country, and year (e.g. wlmde24, wleua21).
  2. Commons Category Tree Harvesting: Traverses structured subcategories including Quality images from [Campaign] and Featured pictures from [Campaign]. (Category Mapping Limitation): Valued Images (VI) are excluded from automated campaign harvesting because Wikimedia Commons does not maintain standardized campaign-level subcategory trees for VI, resulting in non-uniform categorization across contests.
  3. Quality Recognition Outputs:
    • Overall Quality Rate (%): Percentage of submissions achieving formal quality recognition.
    • Awards Breakdown: Itemized counts of Quality Images (QI) and Featured Pictures (FP).
    • Recognized Media Files: Interactive media gallery with lazy-loaded thumbnails, file titles, badge tags, and direct Commons links.
    • Top Contributing Photographers: Distinct accredited photographers whose works received quality designations.

Quality Recognition Rate

Measures the proportion of submitted photographs that earned formal community quality designations:

\[ \text{Quality Recognition Rate} = \frac{\text{Awarded Photos (Quality Images or Featured Pictures)}}{\text{Total Contest Submissions}} \times 100\% \] \[ \text{Recognized Photographers} = \text{Distinct Count of Photographers with At Least One Honored Photo} \]
