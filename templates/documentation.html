Tool 01 Evaluation
Go To Tool →

The Evaluation module computes a multi-dimensional campaign evaluation score (0–100) calibrated dynamically against contemporary regional peer performance:

Step-by-Step Tool Usage & Evaluation Process

  1. Target Campaign Code: Enter the official identifier for the campaign edition you want to evaluate (e.g. wlmbd24 for Wiki Loves Monuments 2024 Bangladesh, or wlede23 for Wiki Loves Earth Germany).
  2. Baseline Reference Edition: Select Previous Year Baseline to measure progression against the immediately preceding year (e.g. 2023 vs 2024), or specify a custom baseline edition for multi-year comparisons. Both cohorts must have active upload records.
  3. Regional Peer Calibration: The tool automatically identifies your country's regional cluster (e.g. South Asia, Northern & Western Europe, ESEAP). Regional benchmarks are computed from active peer campaigns in this group to ensure fair comparison between emerging and veteran communities.
  4. Evaluation Scorecard & Report: Review the 5 dimension scores, qualitative star ratings, quantitative participant counts, and diagnostic insights. Use the Export Report button to generate ready-to-copy summaries for Wikimedia grant reporting, talk pages, or organizing retrospectives.

Evaluation Weighting Model

The composite evaluation score is calculated on a 0–100 scale across five core dimensions organized into complementary structural pairs:

\[ \text{Evaluation Index} = 0.25 S_{\text{ret}} + 0.25 S_{\text{grow}} + 0.20 S_{\text{util}} + 0.15 S_{\text{qual}} + 0.15 S_{\text{div}} \]
  • Retention Index (25%): Percentage of baseline cohort participants who contributed to the target edition. Reflects multi-year community volunteer continuity.
  • Growth Capacity (25%): Percentage of target edition uploaders who are first-time entrants. Measures new contributor recruitment velocity.
  • Content Utility (20%): Share of uploaded images actively integrated into Wikimedia project articles (prop=globalusage). Measures direct encyclopedic deployment.
  • Quality Recognition (15%): Share of submissions awarded formal community quality designations (Quality Images or Featured Pictures).
  • Contributor Diversity (15%): Distribution equity of contributions, measured inversely against the top 10% uploader concentration to evaluate participation breadth.

Empirical Bayesian Shrinkage & Benchmarks

Regional reference thresholds are computed from active peer campaigns within each geographic cluster. To prevent distortion in regions with small sample sizes (\(N \le 3\)), Empirical Bayesian shrinkage blends regional peer metrics with global movement baselines:

\[ B_{\text{effective}} = \frac{N}{N + 3} B_{\text{regional}} + \frac{3}{N + 3} B_{\text{global}} \]
Tool 02 Retention
Go To Tool →

The Retention Analytics interface evaluates participant continuity between successive campaign editions and cross-event contributor migration:

  1. Campaign Selection: Select one or more configured Wiki Loves campaigns (such as Monuments wlm, Earth wle, Folklore wlf, Africa wla, or Bangla wlb). Query codes follow the standard format [event][country][year] (e.g. wlmbd24, wlede22).
  2. Geographic & Temporal Parameters: Select a target country, regional cluster, or all available countries (50+ global nations), and define the longitudinal year span. Use the Replace Codes or Append Codes buttons to populate the target codes input.
  3. Quick Presets: Pre-configured comparison vectors are available in the presets panel for common evaluation pairs (e.g. Monuments Germany, Earth India, Folklore Bangladesh).
  4. Analytical Projection Models: Choose the desired visualization output:
    • Data Table: Tabular breakdown of baseline cohort size, target cohort size, intersecting participant count, and directional retention rate.
    • Heatmap Matrix: Color-coded matrix displaying pairwise retention shares across consecutive editions.
    • Choropleth World Map: Spatial distribution of country-level average or median retention metrics across international campaigns.

Directional Longitudinal Retention

Directional retention from a baseline campaign cohort \(A\) to a subsequent target cohort \(B\) is defined as the proportion of unique contributors in cohort \(A\) who also submitted media in cohort \(B\):

\[ \text{Retention}(A \to B) = \frac{|U_A \cap U_B|}{|U_A|} \times 100\% \]

Where \(U_A\) and \(U_B\) denote the discrete sets of verified upload accounts in each respective category. Retention is non-symmetric: \(\text{Retention}(A \to B) \neq \text{Retention}(B \to A)\) when total cohort sizes differ.

Data Extraction Pipeline & Limitations

Data is extracted in real time directly from Wikimedia Commons. The primary ingestion pathway queries Toolforge replica tools (ptools.toolforge.org) for low-latency category membership enumeration, with automatic failover to the official Wikimedia Commons Action API (commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php) utilizing the categorymembers generator with automatic pagination and exponential backoff.

  • Evaluations reflect observable on-wiki upload records and do not account for offline organizing contributions.
  • Incomplete or unheld campaign categories are identified as missing data and excluded from evaluation to prevent erroneous zero-scoring.
Tool 03 Influx
Go To Tool →

The Influx Analytics module assesses continuous community renewal, newcomer acquisition, and long-term contributor mobilization:

  1. Series Selection: Choose a campaign type (or All Campaigns), country, and consecutive year span (e.g. {{ DEFAULT_START_YEAR }}–{{ CURRENT_YEAR }}).
  2. Generated Campaign Sequence: Chronological campaign sequence automatically assembled or customized to evaluate longitudinal trends.
  3. Trend Visualizations & Outputs:
    • Summary KPI Cards: Cumulative Contributor Pool, Peak Influx Year, Peak Overall Edition, and Contributor Longevity (recurring participants).
    • Visual Influx Dynamics: Dual-axis stacked bar chart showing first-time newcomers (\(I_t\)) stacked with returning veterans (\(R_t\)) alongside the cumulative pool trajectory.
    • Cohort Lifecycle Segmentation: Categorization into 1-Time Entrants (one-off contributors), Repeaters (2–3 editions), and Core Veterans (4+ editions).
    • Detailed Data Table: Comprehensive table with YoY Net Growth, Newcomer Share, Direct Retention, and CSV export capability.

Year-over-Year Newcomer Influx & Retention

For edition \(C_t\) in a chronological series, newcomer influx isolates contributors not seen in any prior recorded edition \(U_{< t}\):

\[ \text{Influx}(C_t) = |U_t \setminus U_{< t}|, \quad \text{Newcomer Share} = \left(\frac{\text{Influx}(C_t)}{|U_t|}\right) \times 100\% \]

Returning veterans are defined as \(R_t = |U_t \cap U_{< t}|\), and direct retention from the immediate prior edition is given by \(\text{Retention}(C_{t-1} \to C_t) = \frac{|U_t \cap U_{t-1}|}{|U_{t-1}|} \times 100\%\).

Multi-Campaign Ecosystem Influx & De-duplication

When analyzing all campaigns together (all[country][year]), participant sets across all active Wiki Loves photography contests in edition year \(t\) are aggregated into a unified ecosystem cohort \(U_t\):

\[ U_t = \bigcup_{e \in E} U_{e, t}, \quad I_t = U_t \setminus \bigcup_{j < t} U_j, \quad R_t = U_t \cap \bigcup_{j < t} U_j \]

Where \(E = \{\text{WLM}, \text{WLE}, \text{WLF}, \text{WLA}, \text{WLB}\}\). Contributor cross-participation across contests is de-duplicated, measuring the total net new individuals brought into the Wikimedia movement annually.

Cumulative Contributor Pool

The cumulative community pool milestone measures the total distinct individuals who have engaged across the evaluated series up to edition year \(t\):

\[ \text{Cumulative Pool}_t = \left|\bigcup_{j \le t} U_j\right| \]
Tool 04 Contributor Mobility
Go To Tool →

The Contributor Mobility module analyzes inter-competition participant fluidity, cross-campaign retention, and community overlap across active photography contests within the same national or regional ecosystem:

  1. Country & Year Selection: Select a target nation and campaign year. The tool identifies all active competitions configured for that country (e.g. WLM, WLE, WLF, WLA, WLB) respecting regional scopes.
  2. Directional Conversion Matrix: An \(N \times N\) matrix displaying the percentage of contributors from each source campaign who transitioned into participating in a target campaign.
  3. Community Similarity (Jaccard Index): Calculates bidirectional community cohesion, measuring the shared contributor core relative to the joint participant union.
  4. Deduplicated Ecosystem Pool: Measures the true net distinct volunteer photographers mobilized across all campaigns in that country during the year.

Directional Conversion Rate Formulation

Directional conversion from source campaign cohort \(A\) to target campaign cohort \(B\) measures the proportion of contributors in \(A\) who also submitted media in \(B\):

\[ \text{Mobility}(A \to B) = \left(\frac{|U_A \cap U_B|}{|U_A|}\right) \times 100\% \]

Because cohort sizes typically differ (\(|U_A| \neq |U_B|\)), directional conversion is non-symmetric: \(\text{Mobility}(A \to B) \neq \text{Mobility}(B \to A)\).

Jaccard Community Similarity

Symmetrical community overlap between two campaigns is quantified using the Jaccard similarity coefficient:

\[ J(A, B) = \left(\frac{|U_A \cap U_B|}{|U_A \cup U_B|}\right) \times 100\% \]

Deduplicated Ecosystem Union

Total distinct volunteer photographers active across any contest within the evaluated country is given by:

\[ U_{\text{ecosystem}} = \left| \bigcup_{e \in E_{\text{scoped}}} U_e \right| \]
Tool 05 Contributor Diversity
Go To Tool →

The Contributor Diversity module evaluates upload volume distribution, contribution inequality, and power-law concentration across contest participants without arbitrary normative baselines:

  1. Campaign & Year Range: Select campaign type (or All Campaigns), target country, and chronological year range.
  2. Normalized Tier Segmentation: Groups participants into four empirical volume tiers: Casual (1–4 uploads), Regular (5–24 uploads), Active (25–99 uploads), and Power (100+ uploads).
  3. Interactive Concentration Trajectory: An interactive line chart plotting the multi-year upload share for any selected top contributor percentile (1%–100%) with automated playback.
  4. Empirical Equality Metrics: Computes the discrete Gini coefficient (\(G \in [0, 1]\)) and Pareto decile shares (\(P_{10}\), \(P_{20}\)).

Discrete Gini Inequality Formulation

For \(n\) active contributors whose individual upload counts \(x_1 \le x_2 \le \dots \le x_n\) are ordered ascendingly, the discrete Gini coefficient is computed as:

\[ G = \frac{2 \sum_{i=1}^n i x_i}{n \sum_{i=1}^n x_i} - \frac{n + 1}{n} \]

\(G = 0\) indicates perfect equality (all participants uploaded identical photo counts), whereas \(G \to 1\) signifies extreme upload concentration among a small cluster of power users.

Pareto Top-Decile Upload Share

The top-decile concentration metric \(P_{10}\) quantifies the percentage of total media submissions uploaded by the top 10% most prolific participants:

\[ P_{10} = \left(\frac{\sum_{i = n - k_{10} + 1}^n x_i}{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}\right) \times 100\%, \quad k_{10} = \max(1, \text{round}(0.10 n)) \]

Longitudinal Percentile Concentration

For any percentile threshold \(p \in [1, 100]\), the cumulative upload share \(S_p\) contributed by the top \(p\%\) of uploaders in edition year \(t\) is given by:

\[ S_p(t) = \left(\frac{\sum_{i = n - k_p + 1}^n x_i}{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}\right) \times 100\%, \quad k_p = \max(1, \text{round}(p \cdot n / 100)) \]
Tool 06 Content Utility
Go To Tool →

The Content Utility module evaluates the direct encyclopedic deployment of campaign media across all Wikimedia projects via live prop=globalusage API queries:

  1. Target Campaign Selection: Choose a campaign, country, and edition year (e.g. wlmde24 or wlmbd23).
  2. Usage Harvesting & Namespace Filtering: Analyzes uploaded files to identify whether they are embedded in encyclopedic articles (Mainspace 0) or user/discussion pages.
  3. Analytics & Output Breakdown:
    • Overall Utility Rate (\(U_r\)): Percentage of uploaded files actively utilized in at least one Wikimedia project.
    • Article Inclusions: Cumulative count of live article instances illustrating encyclopedic knowledge.
    • Cross-Wiki Distribution: Distribution across Wikipedia language editions (e.g. English, German, French, Bengali), Wikidata, Wikivoyage, and Commons galleries.
    • Contributor Impact Leaderboard: Top photographers ranked by total downstream article usage.

Encyclopedic Utility Rate Formulation

For campaign file collection \(F\), let \(P(f)\) denote the set of pages where file \(f\) is actively integrated:

\[ F_{\text{in-use}} = \{ f \in F \mid |P(f)| > 0 \}, \quad \text{Utility Rate } U_r = \left(\frac{|F_{\text{in-use}}|}{|F|}\right) \times 100\% \]

Total article deployment volume is given by \(I_{\text{total}} = \sum_{f \in F} |P(f)|\).

Tool 07 Quality Recognition
Go To Tool →

The Quality Recognition module audits formal community quality designations awarded to campaign submissions by Wikimedia Commons peer-review committees:

  1. Campaign Query: Select target event, country, and year (e.g. wlmde24, wleua21).
  2. Commons Category Tree Harvesting: Traverses structured subcategories including Quality images from [Campaign], Featured pictures from [Campaign], and Valued images from [Campaign].
  3. Quality Recognition Outputs:
    • Overall Quality Rate (\(Q_r\)): Percentage of submissions achieving formal quality recognition.
    • Awards Breakdown: Itemized counts of Quality Images (QI), Featured Pictures (FP), and Valued Images (VI).
    • Recognized Media Files: Interactive media gallery with lazy-loaded thumbnails, file titles, badge tags, and direct Commons links.
    • Top Contributing Photographers: Distinct accredited photographers whose works received quality designations.

Quality Rate Formulation

For campaign upload portfolio \(F\), let \(F_{\text{honored}}\) denote files awarded QI, FP, or VI status:

\[ Q_r = \left(\frac{|F_{\text{honored}}|}{|F|}\right) \times 100\%, \quad \text{Recognized Photographers } H = |\{ \text{uploader}(f) \mid f \in F_{\text{honored}} \}| \]

Metric Glossary & Data Dictionary

Metric / Symbol Formal Definition Operational Description & Scope
Evaluation Index \(0.25 S_{\text{ret}} + 0.25 S_{\text{grow}} + 0.20 S_{\text{util}} + 0.15 S_{\text{qual}} + 0.15 S_{\text{div}}\) Composite 0–100 campaign evaluation score regularized with Empirical Bayesian shrinkage against regional peers.
Directional Retention (\(R_{A \to B}\)) \(\frac{|U_A \cap U_B|}{|U_A|} \times 100\%\) Percentage of distinct uploaders from cohort \(A\) who returned to submit at least one file in subsequent edition \(B\).
Directional Mobility (\(M_{A \to B}\)) \(\frac{|U_A \cap U_B|}{|U_A|} \times 100\%\) Percentage of contributors from a source contest \(A\) who also participated in a target contest \(B\) in the same country.
Jaccard Similarity (\(J\)) \(\frac{|U_A \cap U_B|}{|U_A \cup U_B|} \times 100\%\) Bidirectional community overlap measuring shared participants relative to the joint participant pool.
Discrete Gini Index (\(G\)) \(\frac{2 \sum i x_i}{n \sum x_i} - \frac{n + 1}{n}\) Distribution inequality of media contributions across active photographers on a 0 (equal) to 1 (monopoly) scale.
Top-10% Share (\(P_{10}\)) \(\frac{\sum_{\text{Top } 10\%} x_i}{\sum x_i} \times 100\%\) Share of total submissions uploaded by the most prolific decile of participants (Pareto concentration).
Growth Capacity (\(S_{\text{grow}}\)) \(\frac{|U_{\text{target}} \setminus U_{\text{base}}|}{|U_{\text{target}}|} \times 100\%\) Proportion of target edition uploaders who are first-time entrants relative to the baseline edition.
Content Utility (\(U_r\)) \(\frac{|F_{\text{in-use}}|}{|F_{\text{total}}|} \times 100\%\) Proportion of campaign uploads embedded across Wikipedia articles, Wikidata, Wikivoyage, or sister projects.
Quality Recognition (\(Q_r\)) \(\frac{|F_{\text{honored}}|}{|F_{\text{total}}|} \times 100\%\) Proportion of submissions receiving formal Commons designations (Quality Images, Featured Pictures, Valued Images).
Newcomer Influx (\(I_t\)) \(U_t \setminus \bigcup_{j < t} U_j\) First-time participants in edition \(t\) with no prior upload history in any previous edition of the evaluated contest series.
Cumulative Pool \(\left|\bigcup_{j \le t} U_j\right|\) Total unique contributors ever mobilized by the campaign series through edition year \(t\).
